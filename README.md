## 🎯 Project Overview
 
Employee attrition drives up hiring and training costs and disrupts teams. This project analyses an HR dataset of **1,470 employee records (37 features)** and presents the findings in a single-page, interactive dashboard covering demographics, compensation, tenure, education, and job roles.
 
**Goal:** Identify the key drivers and high-risk segments of attrition and turn them into actionable retention recommendations.

## 🔍 Key Insights
 
- **Early-career employees leave the most:** The **26–35 age group** accounts for **44 of 79 exits (~56%)**, with 18–25 adding another 18.
- **Low pay drives exits:** **58 exits (~73%)** fall in the **up-to-5K salary slab**, versus 19 in 5K–10K, 2 in 10K–15K, and none above 15K.
- **First-year risk:** Attrition peaks at **1 year of tenure (21 exits, ~27%)**, pointing to onboarding and early-engagement gaps.
- **Role hotspots:** **Sales Executive (19), Research Scientist (16), Sales Representative (16), and Laboratory Technician (15)** make up roughly **84%** of all attrition.
- **Education field:** Life Sciences and Medical backgrounds contribute the largest attrition share.
- **Department drill-down:** Slicers for **Human Resources, Research & Development, and Sales** let stakeholders explore each team individually.
---
 
## 💼 Business Impact
 
- Helps HR **pinpoint high-risk segments**: low-salary, early-tenure, younger employees in sales and lab roles.
- Supports **data-driven retention strategies** such as salary benchmarking, stronger first-year onboarding, and clear career-growth paths.
- Replaces manual reporting with a **single-view dashboard** leadership can use to track attrition continuously.
